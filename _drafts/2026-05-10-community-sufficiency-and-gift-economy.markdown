In the US, we have built abundance. Theoretically, no one should suffer and everyone can lead meaningful lives. 
This material abundance took thousands of years of progress. But, not only did this progress mounted itself on the backs
of slavery and wealth inequality. In this pursuit, we all started to feed parts of ourselves to the machine. In this feeding,
we forget that we can call for a timeout, a reexamination: Life could really be meaningful for all of us.

In this feeding, we lost the ability to knock on our neighbor's door for a cup of sugar. 
The concept of community gets held abstractly by a neighborhood committee. The sane and countercultural notion of self-sufficiency is
prefixed with "self" and not "communal". Kids don't really play outside anymore. The village that 
is needed to raise a kid is nowhere to be found.  

In this feeding, we lost the eye for beauty. Developers and HOA and zoning regulations forced dull and uniform constructions onto 
our lush, green sphere. I really think it should be a crime to build the same old building, but I get that cost is usually
the culprit.
